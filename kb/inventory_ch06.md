# Chapter 6 — Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (concept inventory / ground truth)

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Customer-driven marketing strategy (Kotler Figure 7.1): Select customers to serve = Segmentation (divide the total market into smaller segments) + Targeting (select the segment or segments to enter); Decide on a value proposition = Differentiation (differentiate the market offering to create superior customer value) + Positioning (position the market offering in the minds of target customers). Centre: "Create value for targeted customers."
- Figure 6-1 approaches to market segmentation on a broad -> narrow target-market continuum: Mass marketing -> Segment marketing -> Niche marketing -> Individualized marketing.
- Why segment (3 reasons): consumers have a range of different needs; companies have limited amounts of money; marketers need clear consumer insights on their target markets. (Kicking Horse Coffee blend signs; movies aimed at different audiences.)
- Segmentation variables in consumer markets (4): Geographic (where does the target market live and work?), Demographic (what is the basic census-type information on the target market as a whole?), Psychographic (what are the prevailing attitudes, values, interests, habits, and approaches to life this target market shares?), Behaviouristic (how does this target market use and interact with the product?).
- Target market profiles and personas; personas = character descriptions of a brand's typical customer (example persona "Kayla, Planner"; segment "Hotspot Hunters"; "Proud Calgary Enthusiasts").
- Segmentation analytics: research companies (Nielsen, Euromonitor) provide general consumer and lifestyle trend data; other companies (Environics Analytics / PRIZM) provide granular data pinpointing population clusters (e.g., "Middle-Class Mosaic").
- Six-step process for segmenting a market (Figure 6-3): 1 Identify consumer/customer needs and common characteristics in the market; 2 Cluster common consumer/customer variables to create meaningful market segments; 3 Estimate the size and feasibility of each segment; 4 Identify the segment(s) to be targeted; 5 Take actions with marketing programs to reach the segment(s); 6 Monitor and evaluate the success of these programs compared with objectives.
- Socially responsible marketing: social responsibility can be more important than profits if marketing is viewed as exploitative or irresponsible. Legal/ethical issues: marketers must avoid consumer backlash; vulnerable groups; disadvantaged groups; potentially harmful products.
- Business markets use additional bases of segmentation: operating characteristics, purchasing approaches, situational factors, personal characteristics (plus demographics such as industry, company size, location).
- Product positioning: the impression of the branded product you want to establish in consumers' minds relative to their needs and in contrast to the competition. To be successful: all marketing mix efforts must support the positioning strategy; maintain the position through consistent performance and communication; three basic factors — image, product attributes, price.
- Positioning statement: a formalized statement identifying the image a branded product represents in the market and what sets it apart from competition. Template: "For (target market) who desire (target market need), (brand) is the (product category) that offers (product benefits)."
- Positioning example: Chiquita bananas (branding a commodity). Repositioning examples: Spotify, LEGO.
- Repositioning: changing the place a product occupies in consumers' minds relative to competitive products to more accurately meet consumer needs.
- Points-of-parity (POPs): attribute/benefit associations that are not necessarily unique to the brand but may be shared with other brands (Sobeys example). Points-of-difference (PODs): attributes/benefits consumers strongly associate with a brand, positively evaluate, and believe they could not find to the same extent with a competitive brand (Tim Hortons example).
- Differentiation and positioning: choosing a competitive advantage upon which to base positioning — one unique selling proposition (USP) or multiple differences.

## Core terms (textbook)
- Market segmentation: aggregating prospective buyers into groups (segments) that (1) have common needs and (2) will respond similarly to a marketing action. Segments are relatively homogeneous with similar consumption behaviour, attitudes, and target market profiles. Segmentation leads to product differentiation.
- Product differentiation: a strategy that uses different marketing mix activities (product features, advertising) to help consumers perceive a product as different and better than competing products. A firm selling two or more products with different features aimed at different segments is using product differentiation (Hallmark card brands).
- Mass marketing (undifferentiated): one product/one marketing mix for the entire market. Segment marketing (differentiated): different offerings for different segments. Niche marketing: focus on one or a few narrowly defined, specialized segments. Individualized (one-to-one) marketing / mass customization: tailoring goods or services to the tastes of individual customers on a high-volume scale (build-to-order).
- When to segment (ways to segment): one product and multiple market segments (a TV show or magazine appealing to several audiences — low cost, more promotion); multiple products and multiple market segments (different models for different segments — higher costs but better fit); segments of one / mass customization. Segment only when it increases sales revenue, profit, and ROI; if the increased expenses more than offset increased revenues, do NOT segment further.
- Criteria for forming segments (5): (1) potential for increased profit and ROI; (2) similarity of needs of potential buyers WITHIN a segment; (3) difference of needs of buyers AMONG segments; (4) feasibility of a marketing action to reach a segment; (5) simplicity and cost of assigning potential buyers to segments. A tiny allergy segment needing a duplicate salad bar fails "potential for increased profit and ROI."
- Criteria for selecting target segments (5): market size, expected growth, competitive position, cost of reaching the segment, compatibility with the organization's objectives and resources.
- Geographic variables: region, city size, urban/suburban/rural, climate. Demographic: age, gender, income, education, occupation, ethnicity, family life cycle, household size, generation. Psychographic: lifestyle (activities, interests, opinions — AIO), personality, values, attitudes (music taste, "keepers" collector personality = psychographic). Behaviouristic: product features or benefits sought, usage rate (heavy/medium/light/non-users), user status, loyalty status, purchase occasion.
- 80/20 rule: a concept that suggests 80 percent of a firm's sales are obtained from 20 percent of its customers (usage-rate segmentation; "heavy users"; frequency/loyalty programs).
- Usage rate: the quantity consumed or patronage (store visits) during a specific period.
- Market-product grid: a framework relating the market segments of potential buyers (rows) to products offered or marketing actions (columns); cells show estimated market size.
- Target market profile / persona: detailed description (demographics, psychographics, media habits, needs) of the typical customer.
- Perceptual map: a means of displaying or graphing in two dimensions the location of products or brands in the minds of consumers; used to see how consumers perceive competing products and find gaps; repositioning moves a brand on the map. (Practice: coffee ads should compare only to drinks LOWER on the nutrition axis = sugared soft drinks.)
- Product positioning: the place a product occupies in consumers' minds on important attributes relative to competitive products. Two approaches: Head-to-head positioning — competing directly with competitors on similar product attributes in the same target market (Children's Advil vs Children's Tylenol "works faster and longer"). Differentiation positioning — seeking a less-competitive, smaller market niche in which to locate a brand by stressing unique attributes (BMW Streetcarver skateboard with unique stabilizers at $495).
- Repositioning: changing the place a product occupies in a consumer's mind relative to competitive products (Post Grape-Nuts promoted as a topping for yogurt/salad/soup = repositioning, expanding use from breakfast to all day).
- Positioning statement; unique selling proposition (USP); POPs vs PODs.
- Segmentation of business (B2B) markets: demographic (NAICS industry code, number of employees, sales, location), operating characteristics (technology, user status, capabilities), purchasing approaches (centralized vs decentralized, buying criteria, relationships), situational factors (urgency, order size, specific application), personal characteristics (buyer-seller similarity, attitude toward risk, loyalty).

## Instructor practice questions (official, with answers)
1. Hallmark's multiple card brands for different segments = PRODUCT DIFFERENTIATION.
2. If segmentation's added expenses more than offset added revenue -> should NOT attempt to segment its market any further.
3. Seventh Heaven TV show appealing to young children, teens, and older adults = ONE PRODUCT WITH MULTIPLE MARKET SEGMENTS.
4. Allergy salad-bar segment (2-3% of population, duplicate salad bar) has greatest difficulty meeting: POTENTIAL FOR INCREASED PROFIT AND ROI.
5. Universal Concerts: Western Canadians like country music; eastern event has empty seats -> GEOGRAPHIC AND PSYCHOGRAPHIC characteristics (music preference = psychographic interest; east vs west = geographic).
6. Nike Air Jordan "keepers" (buy, keep under bed, show friends) -> PSYCHOGRAPHICS (personality and lifestyle).
7. Children's Advil vs Tylenol "faster and longer" -> HEAD-TO-HEAD POSITIONING.
8. BMW Streetcarver unique stabilizers/wheel design at $495 -> DIFFERENTIATION POSITIONING.
9. Perceptual map coffee ads: compare with SUGARED SOFT DRINKS (the only drink lower on the nutrition axis).
10. Post Grape-Nuts on yogurt/salad/soup -> REPOSITIONING.

## Traps / confusables
- Segmentation (dividing) vs targeting (choosing) vs positioning (placing in minds) vs differentiation (making the offering different).
- Product differentiation (firm-side strategy) vs differentiation positioning (a positioning approach seeking a niche) vs head-to-head positioning.
- Psychographic (lifestyle/personality/values/interests/attitudes) vs behaviouristic (usage rate, benefits sought, loyalty, occasion, user status) vs demographic (age/income/gender/education).
- Repositioning (change an existing position) vs positioning (initial) vs perceptual map (the tool).
- POP (shared, "ticket to play") vs POD (unique, "reason to win").
- Niche marketing (narrow segment) vs individualized/mass customization (segments of one).
- Six-step order: identify needs -> cluster into segments -> estimate size/feasibility -> identify targets -> take action -> monitor/evaluate.
- Criteria for FORMING segments (5) vs criteria for SELECTING target segments (5) — different lists.
