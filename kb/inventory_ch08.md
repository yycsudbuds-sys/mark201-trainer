# Chapter 8 — New Product Development (concept inventory / ground truth)

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Product life cycle (PLC): describes the stages a new product goes through in the marketplace — introduction, growth, maturity, decline; used to help manage a product from initial launch to its eventual decline. Figure 8-1: the sales (units) curve rises through growth, peaks/plateaus in maturity, falls in decline; the profit curve lags, is negative or minimal in introduction, peaks late in growth, levels off in maturity, then declines.
- Figure 8-3 Managing the stages of the PLC (MEMORIZE THE TABLE):
  - Competition: Introduction = few competitors exist; Growth = more competitors enter the market; Maturity = many competitors in the market; Decline = reduced competition, with some competitors leaving the market.
  - General marketing objective: Introduction = Awareness; Growth = Product differentiation; Maturity = Brand loyalty; Decline = Product rationalization.
  - Product: Introduction = focus on a new product or brand; Growth = introduce more features; Maturity = ensure full product line is available and innovative with new ideas; Decline = retain only best sellers or discontinue.
  - Price: Introduction = use a skimming or penetration strategy; Growth = prices are slowly reduced; Maturity = price discounts are used frequently; Decline = very low prices.
  - Place: Introduction = limited distribution; Growth = distribution is increased; Maturity = full distribution is achieved; Decline = distribution is reduced.
  - Promotion: Introduction = focus on building awareness with advertising; Growth = emphasize points of difference versus the competition; Maturity = focus on pricing and sales promotion; Decline = only minimal promotion, if any.
  - Profit: Introduction = minimal profits, if any; Growth = increased profits that reach their maximum; Maturity = maximized profits that level off; Decline = decreasing and minimal profits.
- PLC extension strategies (4): (1) modifying the product, (2) modifying the market, (3) repositioning a product, (4) introducing a new product.
  - Product modifications: product improvements; line extensions (Tide's many varieties — "Why all the varieties? Good idea?" -> risk of cannibalization/confusion).
  - Market modifications: finding new customers; increasing a product's use; creating a new use situation.
  - Repositioning: to meet changing consumer needs, react to competition, or improve value.
- "New products are the lifeblood of a company, helping to make products relevant and to bring future revenues into the company."
- Figure 8-5 Degree of product innovation (MEMORIZE):
  - Minor innovation: requires no new learning by consumers; examples: new and improved toothpaste or detergent; marketing emphasis: gain consumer awareness and wide distribution.
  - Continuous innovation: changes the consumer's normal routine but does not require totally new learning; examples: Swiffer WetJet, wearable technology; marketing emphasis: advertise points of difference and benefits to consumers.
  - Radical innovation: requires new learning and consumption patterns by consumers; examples: Amazon Alexa / Google Home, self-driving cars, 3D printers; marketing emphasis: educate consumers through advertising, product trial, and personal selling; public relations can play a major role.
- Disruptors: Amazon, Uber, Communauto, Airbnb. Notable failures: Amazon Fire Phone, Google Glass, Lululemon's sheer pants, New Coke.
- Figure 8-6 Adoption curve (MEMORIZE percentages and descriptors): Innovators 2.5% (venturesome; higher educated; use multiple information sources); Early adopters 13.5% (leaders in social settings; slightly above average education); Early majority 34% (deliberate; many informal social contacts); Late majority 34% (skeptical; below average social status); Laggards 16% (fear of debt; neighbours and friends are information sources).
- Barriers that deter adoption (4): Usage, Value, Risk, Psychological.
- NPD challenges: expensive undertaking; high risk of failure; high research costs; extensive time and effort on prototypes and marketing materials; product failure means expensive write-offs (labour, materials, expenses to fix the problem); lack of future credibility in the market.
- Figure 8-7 Why new products and services fail — issue -> potential solution: Insignificant point of difference -> determine a distinctive, meaningful POD for the target audience; conduct research with consumers and monitor competitors. Incomplete new concept definition -> identify consumer insights and clearly define the product's features and benefits; develop a clear positioning. Insufficient market attractiveness -> identify a target market (with a need) that is large enough and has growth potential. Poor execution of the marketing mix -> ensure the 4 Ps (incl. brand name and package) are aligned and attractive; focus on gaining sufficient distribution. Bad timing -> launch when consumers are eager to purchase; monitor market conditions and competitor actions.
- Figure 8-8 Strategic approaches to growth (Ansoff matrix; MEMORIZE): Current product + current market = MARKET PENETRATION (finding ways to make current products appeal to current customers). New product + current market = PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT (reaching current customers with a new product). Current product + new market = MARKET DEVELOPMENT (reaching new customers with a current product). New product + new market = DIVERSIFICATION (reaching new customers with a new product).
- Figure 8-9 NPD process (7 steps, in order): 1 New product development strategy; 2 Idea generation; 3 Screening and evaluation; 4 Business analysis; 5 Development; 6 Test marketing; 7 Commercialization.
- Elements in each stage (purpose / marketing information and methods used):
  1 NPD strategy — identify new-product development focus that meets company objectives — company objectives; SWOT analysis of company/product/brand.
  2 Idea generation — brainstorm new ideas — ideas from employees, co-workers, and consumers (also suppliers, competitors, universities, crowdsourcing).
  3 Screening and evaluation — evaluate product ideas and develop concepts — internal evaluation of technical requirements; external concept tests.
  4 Business analysis — identify the product's features and its marketing strategy, and make financial projections — product's key features and anticipated marketing mix; assessment of production, legal, and profitability issues.
  5 Development — create the prototype and test it internally at the company and externally with consumers — internal company assessments; external tests on product prototypes.
  6 Test marketing — test the product and marketing strategy in the marketplace on a limited scale (if necessary) — test marketing in defined areas.
  7 Commercialization — launch and fully market the product in the marketplace — implement all areas of the marketing mix; possible regional rollout.

## Core terms (textbook)
- New product: newness depends on viewpoint — new to the company, new to the market, or new to consumers (degree of learning required).
- PLC shapes/variations: High-learning product — significant consumer education is required; extended introductory period (Swiffer Sweeper when first introduced; early microwave ovens; electric vehicles). Low-learning product — benefits are self-evident, little learning required, short introductory stage, sales begin immediately (a new pizza flavour; new cereal under a known brand); easily copied so strong distribution matters. Fashion product — a style that is popular, declines, then reappears (cycles over years). Fad — rapid rise and rapid fall; very short life (fidget spinners, Pet Rock).
- Length of the PLC varies; technology products have shorter cycles.
- Introduction stage: must first stimulate primary demand (demand for the product class) and later selective demand (demand for a specific brand). Objective: create consumer awareness and stimulate trial. Skimming (high initial price to recover R&D) or penetration (low price to build volume) pricing.
- Growth: rapid sales increase, competitors enter, repeat purchasers appear, product differentiation, more features/versions, broader distribution, profits peak.
- Maturity: slowing sales growth, many competitors, marginal competitors drop out, focus on holding market share and brand loyalty, price discounts and sales promotion, line extensions; profits level off then decline.
- Decline: sales fall due to environmental change or new technology; strategies: DELETION (drop the product from the line) or HARVESTING (keep the product but reduce marketing support/costs to reap minor profits from remaining sales).
- Product modification: altering a product's characteristics (quality, performance, appearance) to increase sales — product improvements. Line extension: adding a new item (flavour, size, variety) to an existing product line (Kellogg's adding two new cereals = line extension; sells quickly because of the known brand).
- Market modification: finding new customers, increasing a product's use (frequency), or creating new use situations (soup recipes to encourage dinner use = new use situation; Arm & Hammer baking soda in the fridge).
- Repositioning (PLC context): reacting to a competitor's position, reaching a new market, catching a rising trend, changing the value offered — trading up (adding value/features and raising price) or trading down (reducing features/quality or price; also downsizing package contents at the same price).
- Cannibalization: when a new product takes sales from a firm's existing products (risk of too many line extensions).
- Adoption curve / diffusion of innovation: how a new product is adopted over time by the five adopter categories.
- Screening and evaluation: internally and externally evaluate new-product ideas to eliminate those that warrant no further effort; reduce the array of brainstormed ideas to a manageable list; concept tests = external evaluations of a product idea with consumers (descriptions, not actual products).
- Business analysis: specify the features of the product and the marketing strategy and make financial projections (profitability, legal, production).
- Development: turn the idea on paper into a prototype; lab and consumer tests.
- Test marketing: offering a product for sale on a limited basis in a defined area to gauge consumer reactions and test marketing strategy; types: standard test markets (actual cities), controlled test markets (paid retailers), simulated/laboratory test markets. Not always necessary (expensive; alerts competitors; not practical for durable goods).
- Commercialization: positioning and launching a new product in full-scale production and sales with full marketing support; regional rollout = introducing the product sequentially into geographical areas to allow production and marketing to build.
- Idea generation sources: employees, co-workers, consumers, suppliers, competitors (reverse engineering), universities/inventors, crowdsourcing/open innovation.

## Traps / confusables
- Market development (existing product, new market) vs product development (new product, existing market) vs diversification (new/new) vs market penetration (existing/existing). Cultural adaptation of an existing line for a new country still = market development.
- Harvesting (keep product, cut marketing support) vs deletion (drop the product).
- High-learning (long introduction) vs low-learning (short introduction) vs fad (fast up, fast down) vs fashion (cyclical).
- Minor innovation (no new learning) vs continuous innovation (changes routine, no totally new learning) vs radical innovation (new learning and consumption patterns).
- Line extension (new item in an existing line) vs brand extension (existing brand into a NEW product class — ch.7) vs product improvement (modify an existing item).
- New use situation vs increasing use (frequency) vs finding new customers (all market modifications).
- Screening and evaluation (narrow ideas; concept tests) vs business analysis (financial projections) vs development (prototype) vs test marketing (limited real market) vs commercialization (full launch).
- Adopter categories: innovators 2.5 / early adopters 13.5 / early majority 34 / late majority 34 / laggards 16. Early adopters are the opinion LEADERS in social settings; innovators are venturesome risk-takers.
- PLC marketing objective by stage: awareness -> product differentiation -> brand loyalty -> product rationalization.
- Profit peaks in GROWTH (reaches maximum) and levels off in MATURITY; sales peak in maturity.
