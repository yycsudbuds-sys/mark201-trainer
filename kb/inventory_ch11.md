# Chapter 11 — Retailing and Wholesaling (concept inventory / ground truth)

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Retailing: includes all activities involved in selling, renting, and providing goods and services to ultimate consumers for personal, family, or household use. Shopping is not only a way to acquire necessities but also a social activity and sometimes an adventure.
- Retailer marketing decisions (list): target market, channels, product assortment, procurement, prices, services, store atmosphere, store activities and experiences, communications, location.
- Retailing mix (Figure — four quadrants around "Target market" and "Consumers", surrounded by "Information"): Products and services mix (variety and assortment, sales functions and assistance, customer service, guarantees and exchanges, product-service trade-offs); Retail pricing mix (image, quality, and value; markups; markdowns; shrinkage); Physical location mix (store location, logistics, size and layout, format, number of stores); Communications mix (personal selling, advertising, displays and signage, public relations, catalogues, telephone sales and follow-up, website, direct marketing, database marketing).
- Target market selection and positioning: selecting a target market is the FIRST item; retail positioning (e.g., "buy Canadian?" — Canadian Tire); shopper marketing.
- Products and services: level of service and mix — prepurchase, postpurchase, ancillary services (e.g., experiences, dining — West Edmonton Mall water park).
- Merchandise mix: breadth = number of different product lines carried (shoes, appliances, CDs, men's clothing); depth = number of items within each product line. Store atmosphere provides the overall impression.
- Pricing: markup, markdown; off-price retailing. Physical location: where to locate; number of stores; non-store (e.g., mail).
- Communications: to communicate unique benefits; use of integrated, multiple channels (Indigo example).
- Online retailing: allows search, evaluation (comparison), purchase; reasons for shopping online include convenience, choice, communication, customization, cost, control (the six Cs); reviews create smarter, more informed, and demanding consumers; influencers (opinions) are important.
- Modern retail environment: face-to-face (F2F) not dead yet (instant gratification, convenience, experience, savings); omnichannel, social media purchasing; decline of middle-market retailers; role of technology; consumers dislike long lines, poor customer service, stock-outs; retail optimization (Shopify).
- Retail trends (Retail Council of Canada / Adyen 2025 survey): mobile banking; nearly two-thirds of online shoppers reach "checkout" then leave to compare shipping costs and prices on other sites (cart abandonment threat); 9% use digital wallet, most use debit/credit card; improve online retail experience via experiential/interactive activities (online social shopping such as Instagram storefronts, QR codes, GPS).
- Wholesaling: includes all the activities in selling goods or services to those who buy for resale or business use.
- Wholesaler types (Figure, MEMORIZE): Merchant wholesalers — Full service (general merchandise wholesalers; specialty merchandise wholesalers) and Limited-service (rack jobbers; cash and carry wholesalers; drop shippers; truck jobbers). Agents and brokers — manufacturers' agents; selling agents; brokers. Manufacturer's branches and offices — branch office; sales office.
- Wholesaler functions (9): selling and promoting; buying and assortment building; bulk breaking; warehousing; transportation; financing; risk bearing; market information; management services and counselling.

## Core terms (textbook)
- Value of retailing: creates time, place, form, and possession utilities (e.g., long hours = time utility; convenient locations = place utility; assembling/altering products = form utility; credit/layaway = possession utility).
- Classifying retail outlets by FORM OF OWNERSHIP: Independent retailer (owned by an individual; most common form; one store). Corporate chain (multiple outlets under common ownership; centralized buying; bargaining power — Loblaws, Canadian Tire corporate). Contractual system — independently owned stores that band together to act like a chain: retailer-sponsored cooperatives (Home Hardware), wholesaler-sponsored voluntary chains (IGA), franchises (Tim Hortons, McDonald's; franchisor provides name/system; franchisee pays fee + royalty).
- Classifying by LEVEL OF SERVICE: Self-service (customer performs most functions; warehouse clubs, gas stations, online). Limited-service (some services such as credit and returns; Walmart, department stores). Full-service (many services; personalized, specialty stores, luxury department stores — Holt Renfrew).
- Classifying by MERCHANDISE LINE: Depth of line — specialty outlets / single-line stores (deep assortment in a narrow line: Lululemon, Foot Locker); category killers (specialty discount outlets focusing on one product category at very low prices with deep assortment: Best Buy, Staples, Home Depot). Breadth of line — general merchandise stores (department stores carry many lines); scrambled merchandising — offering several unrelated product lines in a single store (drugstore selling groceries; Canadian Tire selling food); hypermarket / supercentre — very large store combining general merchandise and grocery (Walmart Supercentre, Real Canadian Superstore). Intertype competition — competition between very dissimilar types of retail outlets caused by scrambled merchandising (Walmart vs Loblaws selling groceries).
- Non-store retailing: occurs outside a retail outlet through activities involving varying levels of customer and retailer involvement: automatic vending; direct mail and catalogues; television home shopping; online retailing; telemarketing; direct selling (door-to-door, in-home parties: Avon, Tupperware). Traditional "bricks-and-mortar" stores engaging in non-store retailing become "click-and-mortar."
- Retailer marketing decisions: (1) Target market selection and retail positioning — retail positioning matrix positions retailers on two dimensions: breadth of product line (narrow vs broad) and value added (low vs high: location, reliability, prestige, service). Four quadrants: broad line/high value added (department stores like Holt Renfrew/Hudson's Bay), broad line/low value added (Walmart, Costco), narrow line/high value added (specialty/luxury like Tiffany), narrow line/low value added (Payless/Dollarama style). Retail positioning = how the retailer wants to be perceived; a high-end retailer carrying cheap suits = wrong retail positioning. Shopper marketing — a discipline designed to understand how consumers behave as shoppers in different channels and formats; extends to the time and place a consumer first thinks about purchasing; by necessity multichannel (traditional media, new media, direct marketing, loyalty, trade promotion, in-store).
- (2) Retailing mix: Products and services (merchandise mix = how many different types of products a store carries and in what assortment; level of service; store atmosphere/atmospherics — layout, music, lighting, scent; retail image); Retail pricing (markup; markdown = reducing price to move slow merchandise; shrinkage = theft/breakage/paperwork errors; off-price retailing = selling brand-name merchandise at lower than regular prices via buying excess inventory: warehouse clubs (Costco), outlet stores, single-price/extreme-value stores (Dollarama), off-price chains (Winners)); Physical location (central business district — downtown; regional shopping centres — 50-150 stores with anchor department stores; community shopping centres — one primary store + 20-40 smaller; strip location — cluster of stores on a busy street; power centre — big strip with several national anchors/category killers; lifestyle centre — open-air upscale); Communications (integrated, multiple channels to communicate unique benefits; store image also communicates).
- Category management: assigning a manager responsibility for the profitability of a product category (e.g., shoes) and managing it as a strategic business unit; aligns assortment with target market.
- Multichannel retailers: use and integrate a combination of traditional store and non-store formats (store, catalogue, online); synergy of sharing information among channel operations; cross-channel shopper — a consumer who researches online then buys in store (or vice versa: showrooming = view in store, buy online; webrooming = research online, buy in store).
- Online retailing benefits (six Cs): convenience (24/7, anywhere), choice (broad selection, assistance), communication (email, chat, reviews, social media), customization (Dell configure-your-own computer), cost (lower prices, comparison), control (empowered consumers decide at their own pace, on their own terms).
- Wheel of retailing: a concept that describes how new forms of retail outlets enter the market as low-status, low-margin, low-price outlets, then gradually add services and raise prices/status, leaving room for new low-price entrants (McDonald's adding seating, drive-thru, salads; discount stores -> department-store-like).
- Retail life cycle: the process of growth and decline that retail outlets, like products, experience — early growth, accelerated development, maturity, decline (market share rises then falls).
- Wholesaling detail: Merchant wholesalers — independently owned firms that TAKE TITLE to the merchandise they handle. Full-service: general merchandise wholesalers (broad range of products, little depth) and specialty merchandise wholesalers (narrow range, deep assortment). Limited-service: rack jobbers (furnish and display racks/products in stores, own the inventory, bill only for items sold — e.g., hosiery, magazines); cash and carry wholesalers (customers pay cash and furnish their own transportation; no credit or delivery); drop shippers / desk jobbers (own the goods but never physically handle, store, or deliver them; ship from the producer directly to the customer — coal, lumber, chemicals); truck jobbers (small wholesalers with a small warehouse; sell perishables for cash from the truck — bakery, dairy, meat). Agents and brokers — do NOT take title; earn commissions: manufacturers' agents / representatives (work for several producers, carry noncompetitive lines in an exclusive territory); selling agents (represent a single producer and are responsible for the entire marketing function of that producer); brokers (independent firms whose principal function is to bring buyers and sellers together to make sales; usually no continuous relationship — real estate, food, insurance). Manufacturer's branches and offices — owned by the manufacturer (vertical integration in wholesaling): branch office carries inventory and performs wholesaler functions; sales office does NOT carry inventory, performs only a sales function.

## Instructor practice questions (official, with answers)
1. High-end men's clothier carries low-quality, low-priced suits in a recession and loses loyal customers = wrong RETAIL POSITIONING.
2. Boutique adds well-trained salespeople, personalized experience, customer preference database = move from LIMITED SERVICE TO FULL SERVICE.
3. Multichannel retailer lets customers order out-of-stock items via catalogue = benefits from the SYNERGY OF SHARING INFORMATION among different channel operations.
4. Researching, comparing, deciding on a camera online at own pace = EMPOWERED CONSUMERS (control).
5. Dell lets shoppers choose computer components = CUSTOMIZATION benefit of online shopping.
6. TRUE of non-store retailing: comprises activities of varying levels of customer and retailer involvement, outside the store environment.
7. Merchandise mix = how many different products (types) a store carries, and in what assortment.
8. All activities involved in selling, renting, providing goods and services to ultimate consumers for personal, family, household use = RETAILING.
9. Independent lighting retailers concentrate buying power and plan collaborative promotion/pricing = CONTRACTUAL SYSTEM.
10. Use of traditional media, new media, direct marketing, loyalty, trade promotion to understand when/where customers first think about purchasing = SHOPPER MARKETING.

## Traps / confusables
- Retailing (to ultimate consumers for personal use) vs wholesaling (to those who buy for resale or business use).
- Form of ownership (independent, corporate chain, contractual system) vs level of service (self, limited, full) vs merchandise line (depth/breadth) — three separate classification schemes.
- Contractual system (independents banding together: co-ops, voluntary chains, franchises) vs corporate chain (common ownership).
- Specialty outlet / category killer (deep, narrow) vs general merchandise/department store (broad) vs scrambled merchandising (unrelated lines) vs hypermarket.
- Retail positioning matrix dimensions: breadth of product line × value added (NOT price × quality).
- Merchandise mix (types + assortment) vs store atmosphere vs level of service (all part of products & services mix).
- Off-price retailing (brand names below regular price via excess inventory: Winners, Costco, outlets) vs markdown (temporary reduction on own slow stock) vs discount store (everyday low margins).
- Six Cs of online shopping: convenience vs choice vs communication vs customization vs cost vs control — scenario questions hinge on the specific benefit.
- Shopper marketing (how consumers behave as shoppers across channels; multichannel) vs retail positioning vs category management.
- Merchant wholesalers (take title) vs agents/brokers (no title; commission) vs manufacturer's branches/offices (owned by manufacturer; branch = inventory, sales office = none).
- Rack jobber (owns racks/inventory, bills for sold items) vs drop shipper (title but never handles) vs cash-and-carry (no credit/delivery) vs truck jobber (perishables from truck).
- Manufacturers' agent (several non-competing producers, territory, part of marketing) vs selling agent (one producer, entire marketing function) vs broker (brings buyers and sellers together, no ongoing relationship).
- Wheel of retailing (entry low-price -> upgrade -> leaves gap) vs retail life cycle (growth/decline over time).
- Power centre vs regional shopping centre vs community shopping centre vs strip vs lifestyle centre vs central business district.
