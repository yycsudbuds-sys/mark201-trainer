# Chapter 1 — Understanding Marketing (concept inventory / ground truth)
Textbook: Kerin et al., Marketing: The Core, 7th Canadian ed. (McGraw Hill, 2024). Instructor: slides "MARK 201 - Ch01-2026" + Intro Lecture.

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Marketing is often misunderstood as just "ads or selling"; its practices and theories are mostly invisible to the average consumer.
- Definitions given on slides:
  - Canadian Marketing Association (CMA): "a set of business practices designed to plan for and present an organization's products or services in ways that build effective customer relationships."
  - Chartered Institute of Marketing (UK): "the management process responsible for identifying, anticipating and satisfying customer requirements profitably."
  - Textbook/AMA style: marketing is the activity for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that benefit the organization, its stakeholders, and society at large.
- "Who's in charge? The consumer? The marketer?" -> both: marketing discovers needs but also shapes wants.
- Role of marketing (figure): the marketing department sits among other departments (R&D, manufacturing, finance, HR, IT, senior management) and links the organization to society: alliances with other organizations, partnerships with suppliers, relationships with customers, ownership by shareholders/owners; all within environmental forces (social, economic, technological, competitive, regulatory).
- Lululemon case (Intro lecture): well-known brand, four CEOs in five years, plan to lay off 1,100, skeptical customers, changing preferences, unionization demands, flat sales -> marketer's role is to make key decisions to stop declining performance (back to basics, stress key positive attributes, leverage brand equity, change the business operating model).
- The marketing process (Figure 1-2): (1) Identify consumer needs (discover needs by researching what consumers' needs are), (2) Manage the marketing mix to meet consumer needs (satisfy needs by designing a marketing program with the right combination of product, price, promotion, place), (3) Realize profits for a company (or objectives for non-profit organizations). Information about needs flows from the market (potential consumers) to the organization; products/services/ideas flow back. "A process that focuses on consumer needs."
- CSR initiatives (three types on slide): (1) sponsorship and/or spearheading of community programs; (2) sponsorship and/or involvement in fundraising initiatives for charitable organizations; (3) a business philosophy that implements socially responsible business practices.
- New and evolving marketing practices (7): (1) digital marketing, including content marketing, mobile marketing, and social media marketing; (2) augmented reality; (3) experiential marketing; (4) influencer marketing; (5) partnership marketing; (6) metrics and analytics; (7) new marketing regulations and ethical considerations (CMA Canadian Marketing Code of Ethics and Standards; many companies, industries and professional associations have codes of ethics).
- Who markets? Everyone — goods, services, ideas; for-profit and non-profit, individuals, places, causes.
- Marketing and your career: marketing affects all individuals, all organizations, all industries, all countries; has many applications that affect our lives; this knowledge should make us better customers, more informed citizens, better career planners.

## Core terms and definitions (textbook)
- Marketing: the process of planning goods, services, or ideas to meet consumer needs and organizational objectives; includes the conception of products and their pricing, promotion, and distribution. Effective marketing requires intimate knowledge and understanding of consumers and the marketplace (more than common sense).
- Exchange: the trade of things of value between a buyer and a seller so that each is better off after the trade. Exchange does NOT require money (donating blood to the Red Cross in exchange for a feeling of satisfaction IS a marketing exchange).
- Four factors required for marketing to occur: (1) two or more parties (individuals or organizations) with unsatisfied needs; (2) a desire and ability on their part to be satisfied; (3) a way for the parties to communicate; (4) something to exchange.
- Need: occurs when a person feels deprived of basic necessities such as food, water, clothing, shelter (physiological deprivation). Want: a need that is shaped by a person's knowledge, culture, and personality (a learned preference for a specific item that satisfies the need). Marketers do not create needs; they shape wants. Exam pattern: an ad appeals to a NEED (thirst) and shapes a WANT (Sunny Delight).
- Market: people with both the desire and the ability to buy a specific product.
- Target market: one or more specific groups (segments) of existing and potential consumers toward which an organization directs its marketing program/efforts. Rationale: with limited funds it is better to channel resources to the specific groups most interested in purchasing rather than target everyone.
- Marketing mix / 4 Ps: product, price, place, promotion — the controllable factors a marketing manager uses to solve a marketing problem; must be consistently coordinated and appeal to the target market. Product = a good, service, or idea to satisfy needs; Price = what is exchanged for the product; Promotion = a means of communication between seller and buyer; Place = a means of getting the product to the consumer.
- Environmental forces: the uncontrollable factors — social, economic, technological, competitive, regulatory — that affect a marketing decision.
- Customer value: the unique combination of benefits received by targeted buyers that includes quality, price, convenience, on-time delivery, and both before-sale and after-sale service. Firms deliver value via best price, best product, or best service.
- Value proposition: the unique combination of benefits a brand promises to deliver to consumers; it differentiates brands (slide examples: Ancient Grains Crust pizza = exchange/benefit; MEC Buyer's Guide = product quality).
- Marketing program: a plan that integrates the marketing mix to provide a good, service, or idea to prospective buyers. After discovering consumer needs, marketing's next task is to translate information about consumer needs into products that satisfy them.
- Customer relationship management (CRM): the process of building and developing long-term relationships with customers by delivering customer value and satisfaction. Relationship marketing: linking the organization to its individual customers, employees, suppliers, and other partners for their mutual long-term benefit.
- Customer experience management: managing the internal response customers have to all aspects of an organization and its offering.
- Marketing concept: the idea that an organization should (1) strive to satisfy the needs of consumers (2) while also trying to achieve the organization's goals. Market orientation: an organization that focuses its efforts on (1) continuously collecting information about customers' needs and competitors' capabilities, (2) sharing this information across departments, and (3) using it to create customer value.
- Evolution of business philosophies / orientations: Production orientation (focus on producing goods efficiently; demand exceeded supply; early 1900s) -> Sales orientation (focus on selling/promotion to move output; competition grew; 1930s-1950s) -> Marketing orientation / marketing concept era (focus on consumer needs; 1950s-60s onward) -> Relationship marketing / societal marketing orientation (long-term customer relationships plus social responsibility; today). Societal marketing concept: the view that organizations should satisfy the needs of consumers in a way that also provides for society's well-being. Corporate social responsibility (CSR): the idea that organizations are part of a larger society and are accountable to that society for their actions.
- Ultimate consumers: the people who use the goods and services purchased for a household. Organizational buyers: manufacturers, wholesalers, retailers, and government agencies that buy goods and services for their own use or for resale (a store owner buying signs to resell = organizational buyer; a parent buying milk = ultimate consumer).
- Utility: the benefits or customer value received by users of the product. Four types: form (production of the good or service), place (having the offering available where consumers need it), time (having it available when needed), possession (getting the product to the consumer so it can be used; e.g., credit, delivery). Marketing creates place, time, and possession utility and influences form utility.
- Macromarketing: the aggregate flow of a nation's goods and services to benefit society. Micromarketing: how an individual organization directs its marketing activities and allocates its resources to benefit its customers.
- Ethics: the moral principles and values that govern the actions and decisions of an individual or group; codes of ethics (CMA).
- Marketing of ideas, places, people, causes (Red Cross blood donation; Ministry of Transportation safety campaign) — non-profits and governments market too.

6. Continuously collecting info about customers' needs and competitors' capabilities, sharing across departments, using it to create value = MARKET ORIENTATION.
7. What kinds of organizations engage in marketing? EVERY organization markets.

## Common traps / confusables
- Need vs want (marketers shape wants; they don't create needs).
- Target market vs market vs mass market (segmentation precedes targeting).
- Marketing mix (controllable 4 Ps) vs environmental forces (uncontrollable).
- Marketing concept vs market orientation vs societal marketing concept vs CSR.
- Customer value vs value proposition vs customer satisfaction.
- Ultimate consumer vs organizational buyer (purpose of purchase: personal/household use vs resale/business use).
- Exchange need not involve money.
- Selling/advertising is only part of promotion, which is only one of the 4 Ps.
- Production -> sales -> marketing -> relationship/societal orientation (chronological order).
