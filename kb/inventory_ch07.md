# Chapter 7 — Products and Brands (concept inventory / ground truth)

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Product: a good, a service, or an idea consisting of a bundle of tangible and intangible attributes that satisfies consumers' needs and is received in exchange for money or something else of value.
- Total product concept (Figure 7-2, bicycle example): three layers — CORE (benefits sought: provides transportation and leisure activity), ACTUAL (the physical product including branding, design, and features: branded bicycle, metal unisex frame, 10 speeds, aluminum alloy wheels, padded seat), AUGMENTED (additional features or benefits that come with the actual product: warranty, repair facilities, online FAQs, service videos). "Marketers view products as having three different layers." Customers ultimately buy BENEFITS (core).
- Consumer products (4): Convenience, Shopping, Specialty, Unsought. "The manner in which a consumer product is classified depends on the individual."
- Business products: PRODUCTION goods (raw materials, component parts) and SUPPORT goods (installations, accessory equipment, supplies, services). DERIVED DEMAND: demand for business products is derived from demand for consumer products (Ford car demand up -> demand for paint-spraying equipment up).
- Brand: a name, phrase, symbol, or design, or a combination, uniquely given by a company to a product to distinguish it from the competition; needs to be developed and nurtured by marketing activity (Lululemon, MADD examples).
- Product line: length and depth. Product mix: width (P&G example: baby care, fabric and home care, beauty, health and grooming lines — Pampers, Bounce, Tide, Head & Shoulders, Old Spice).
- Difference between brand and product: substance = authenticity; important to understand a brand's key associations (H&M, Gap, Zara). Brands provide an "educational" opportunity — teach people why they should care; perceptions matter for brand relationships (Endy mattress).
- Managing brands as assets: Interbrand 2025 leading financial brands (Apple #1, Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Samsung, Toyota, Coca-Cola, Instagram, McDonald's, Mercedes-Benz, Cisco, Louis Vuitton, YouTube, BMW, NVIDIA...); Leger most admired brands in Canada. If brands are assets, marketers must INVEST money to maintain their value.
- Experiential branding: not to sell you something, but to show how a brand can enrich a consumer's life; storytelling is essential.
- Brand loyalty: favourable attitudes that a consumer has over time toward a brand; "Marketers work to associate brands with specific personality traits and to help consumers make emotional connections with their brands" (KPMG 2019 truth about customer loyalty).
- Types of brands: Manufacturer brands, Private-label brands (Canadian Tire example), Generic.
- Uniqueness of services — the four I's: Intangibility, Inconsistency, Inseparability, Inventory. Inventory: services cannot necessarily be stored and accessed when in demand; issues arise due to fluctuating demand; difficulty assessing requirements needed to service customers at peak times; IDLE PRODUCTION CAPACITY = supply of service exceeds demand; expensive.

## Core terms (textbook)
- Product item: a specific product with a unique brand, size, or price; SKU = stock keeping unit (unique identifier for each distinct item).
- Product line: a group of products that are closely related because they satisfy a class of needs, are used together, are sold to the same customer group, are distributed through the same outlets, or fall within a given price range (Build-a-Bear teddy bears for gift-giving = product line). Product line DEPTH = number of product items within a line; product line LENGTH = total number of items; Product mix: all the product lines offered by a company; product mix WIDTH (breadth) = number of different lines.
- Consumer products classified by effort and risk:
  - Convenience: frequently purchased, inexpensive, minimal shopping effort, widespread distribution, mass advertising, brand aware but will accept substitutes (toothpaste, snacks, gum).
  - Shopping: compared on price, quality, style; fewer outlets, moderate price, differentiation emphasized, limited brand preference (clothing, appliances, cameras, furniture).
  - Specialty: unique characteristics, consumer makes a significant effort to search for and buy; exclusive/very limited distribution; very brand loyal; high price (Rolex, luxury cars, designer goods).
  - Unsought: products the consumer does not know about or knows about but does not initially want; aggressive promotion and personal selling (life insurance, burial plots, radical new products).
- Business products: Production goods become part of the final product (raw materials like grain or lumber; component parts like engines or microchips). Support goods assist in production: installations (buildings, fixed equipment), accessory equipment (tools, office equipment), supplies (stationery, paper clips, brooms), industrial services (maintenance, repair, legal, consulting). Derived demand.
- Services: intangible activities or benefits an organization provides to satisfy consumers' needs in exchange for money or something else of value. Four I's: Intangibility (cannot be touched or seen before purchase -> use tangible cues, testimonials, facilities); Inconsistency (quality varies with the people who deliver it -> standardization, training); Inseparability (the consumer cannot separate the deliverer of the service from the service itself; production and consumption occur simultaneously); Inventory (perishable, cannot be stored; idle production capacity; use pricing, reservations, part-time staff to smooth demand).
- Brand name: the part of a brand that can be spoken (words, letters, numbers). Brand mark / logo: the part that cannot be spoken (symbol, design). Trademark: legal protection identifying that the owner has the exclusive right to use the brand name or mark (Canadian Intellectual Property Office; registered for 10 years, renewable). Copyright: legal protection of original written works, sound recordings, or forms of communication from being copied (covers music, literature, performances); course material states protection = creator's lifetime plus 50 years (note: Canada extended copyright to life + 70 years effective Dec 30, 2022 — the course/test-bank answer is "lifetime plus 50 years"). Patent: protects inventions.
- Brand equity: the added value a brand name brings to a product beyond its functional benefits; provides a competitive advantage and allows higher prices; brands are assets to be invested in.
- Brand personality: a set of human characteristics associated with a brand name.
- Brand loyalty: a favourable attitude toward and consistent purchase of a single brand over time.
- Brand advocate: a person who speaks well of a product by passing along positive comments to other people (word of mouth, reviews).
- Brand associations / brand image: the thoughts and feelings linked to a brand in memory; brand promise.
- Brand licensing: a contractual agreement whereby a company allows another firm to use its brand name, patent, trade secret, or other property for a royalty or fee.
- Branding strategies:
  - Manufacturer (national) branding — the producer owns the brand name (Tide, Nike).
  - Private-label (store/house) branding — the retailer or wholesaler owns the brand, usually made by a manufacturer (President's Choice, Kirkland, Canadian Tire's Mastercraft/Motomaster).
  - Generic branding — no brand name; plain packaging; low price.
  - Multiproduct (family/umbrella/corporate) branding — one name for all products (Samsung, Heinz); enables brand extensions and sub-branding (combining a corporate/family name with a new brand name: Porsche Cayenne, Gatorade G2).
  - Brand extension: using a current brand name to enter a NEW product class (Dove soap -> Dove shampoo; Tide detergent -> Tide stain remover).
  - Multibranding — a different brand name for each product or segment (P&G: Tide, Gain, Cheer; Black & Decker vs DeWalt).
  - Mixed branding — a firm markets products under its own name AND under a reseller's (private-label) name.
  - Co-branding — pairing two brand names of two manufacturers on a single product (Tim Hortons + Oreo doughnut; Nike + Apple).
- Packaging: any container in which a product is offered for sale and on which label information is conveyed. Benefits: (1) functional benefits — convenience, storage, protection, usability (Tide PODS single-use pods = functional benefit); (2) communication benefits — label information, directions, nutrition, brand benefits; (3) perceptual benefits — shapes perception of quality, helps the brand stand out on the shelf. Labelling: integral part of packaging. Warranty: a statement indicating the liability of the manufacturer for product deficiencies (part of the augmented product).
- Experiential branding; storytelling; brands as assets (invest to maintain value).

## Instructor practice questions (official, with answers)
1. Massage centre puts customer testimonials on its website -> dealing with INTANGIBILITY.
2. Three-year warranty for the first 50 buyers of the Spectra car -> AUGMENTED product.
3. Patagonia repairs zippers/torn clothing long after purchase -> AUGMENTED product.
4. Legally protects the written word, a sound recording, or a form of communication from being copied -> COPYRIGHTS (Canada: lifetime + 50 years per course material).
5. Tide PODS convenient single-use pods, no measuring -> packaging provides a FUNCTIONAL BENEFIT.
6. Under the total product concept, what do customers buy? THE BENEFITS OF THE PRODUCT (core).
7. A person who speaks well of a product, passing comments to others = BRAND ADVOCATE.
8. If brands are assets, marketers must INVEST MONEY TO MAINTAIN THEIR VALUE.
9. Build-a-Bear teddy bears designed as gifts for every occasion = PRODUCT LINE (closely related; satisfy gift-giving need).
10. Ford car demand up -> demand for paint-spraying equipment up = DERIVED DEMAND.

## Traps / confusables
- Core vs actual vs augmented (warranty, repair service, delivery, installation, website FAQs = augmented; features/design/brand/packaging/quality level = actual; fundamental benefit = core).
- Convenience vs shopping vs specialty vs unsought — classification depends on the individual consumer's effort and behaviour, not the product itself.
- Product line (length/depth) vs product mix (width/breadth) vs product item/SKU.
- Trademark (brand name/mark) vs copyright (creative works) vs patent (inventions).
- Brand equity (added value) vs brand loyalty (attitude + repeat purchase) vs brand personality (human traits) vs brand image/associations.
- Manufacturer vs private-label vs generic; multiproduct/family vs multibranding; brand extension vs sub-branding vs co-branding vs licensing vs mixed branding.
- Inseparability (producer and consumer together; simultaneous) vs inconsistency (variable quality) vs intangibility (cannot see/touch) vs inventory (perishability; idle production capacity).
- Production goods (become part of the product: raw materials, component parts) vs support goods (installations, accessory equipment, supplies, services).
- Derived demand belongs to BUSINESS products (B2B demand derived from consumer demand).
