# Chapter 12 — Integrated Marketing Communications: The Mix, Trends, and Industry (concept inventory / ground truth)

## Slide-emphasized points (instructor)
- Communication: the process of conveying a message to others; requires a source (sender), a message (information to be transmitted), a channel of communication, a receiver (consumers who read, hear, or see the message), and the processes of encoding and decoding. (Textbook also: feedback loop — response and feedback; noise — extraneous factors that distort; field of experience — shared understanding between sender and receiver needed for effective communication.)
- "The lines are blurred between reality, entertainment, self-expression, and marketing communications." (Beckham/Stella example video.)
- Common barriers to effective communication (4): poor awareness; selective inattention; selective inexposure; personal bias (e.g., principles).
- Marketing communications (Kotler/Keller): "the means by which firms attempt to inform, persuade, and remind consumers about the products and brands they sell."
- Integrated marketing communications (IMC): the concept of designing marketing communications programs that coordinate all promotional activities to provide a consistent message across all audiences / to a target audience. Of utmost importance: all marketing communications must work together in an integrated fashion and rely on metrics and analytics to measure and evaluate success and make improvements.
- IMC — marketer must consider two issues: (1) balance of the elements; (2) coordinate all promotional activities to provide a consistent message to a target audience (consumer or intermediary or both). Use a process that makes it easy to design and evaluate.
- IMC components (Pearson figure "Concept of Integrated Marketing Communications"): Advertising, PR/Publicity, Customer Service, Direct Marketing, Personal Selling, Sponsorship, Digital/Social Media, Email, Sales Promotion. IMC example: Tampa Bay Rays Friday Fest (tickets, promotion, collectible T-shirt, website, phone line).
- Figure 12-1 The Customer Advocacy Funnel (top to bottom; MEMORIZE order): Awareness (a company trying to raise online product awareness may use a website, search engine optimization, online video, and display ads to drive consumers to an online destination) -> Interest (interesting product attributes are highlighted to initiate potential customers to learn more) -> Engagement (potential customers are invited to participate in the product experience and interact with its marketing) -> Trial (customers obtain free samples or purchase the product as a limited trial or download) -> Purchase (positive product experiences lead to product purchase) -> Loyalty (ongoing positive product experiences lead to repeat purchases; encouraged through CRM/loyalty programs that reward continued purchases) -> Advocacy (loyal customers are rewarded with additional experiences and become advocates who recommend the product to others).
- Figure 12-2 Push and pull communication strategies: PUSH strategy — focuses on distributors, wholesalers, retailers (the channel); uses direct response, personal selling, trade promotions (trade allowances, trade shows, merchandising allowances, co-op advertising funds) to gain results such as listings, sales, merchandising displays, feature pricing, inclusion in flyers. PULL strategy — focuses on consumers, media, public; uses advertising, publicity, consumer promotion, product placement, event marketing, social media and sponsorship, personal selling to get results such as awareness, interest, recognition, trial, purchase, loyalty, advocacy. "Work together."
- Figure 12-3 Steps in the marketing communications process (6, MEMORIZE order): 1 Specify the IMC objectives; 2 Identify the target audience (consider customer touch points); 3 Set the promotional budget; 4 Design the promotional program (consider the product life cycle); 5 Schedule and run the IMC elements; 6 Evaluate the program and recommend changes.
- Figure 12-4 Product life cycle considerations for promotional programs: Introduction — promotional objective TO INFORM; activities: publicity in magazines, advertising, salesforce calling on intermediaries, sales promotion in the form of free samples. Growth — TO PERSUADE; personal selling to intermediaries, advertising to differentiate a product from competing brands. Maturity — TO REMIND; reminder advertising, sales promotion in the form of discounts, coupons, and events, limited personal selling, direct-mail reminders. Decline — TO PHASE OUT; little money spent on promotion.
- Objectives: Communication objectives — lay the foundation to build customer relationships (awareness, knowledge, attitude). Behavioural objectives — relate to outcomes that are action-oriented and necessary for business success (trial, purchase, repeat purchase).
- Inbound vs outbound communications: Inbound — requires excellent content and the ability to be seen in the right places (search, social, content marketing; consumers come to you); can be considerably more cost-effective than outbound. Outbound — considered more traditional (advertising, cold calls, direct mail; pushed out to audiences).
- Digital channels (Keller/Swaminathan): PAID — search advertising, display advertising, social media advertising, e-mail marketing, paid bloggers/influencers, mobile in-app advertising. OWNED — company websites, company-owned social media (e.g., Facebook pages, YouTube channel), mobile apps. EARNED — review websites (Amazon reviews, Yelp), public relations, media coverage.
- Changing landscape (trends): Connected consumers — affordable and fast Internet technology provides easy-to-use services and devices that facilitate marketing communications. Evolving media — content produced strictly for streaming; traditional media creating their own online assets to remain competitive; news organizations use YouTube channels and apps; radio stations stream content and create podcasts; out-of-home being replaced with digital boards; digital advertising has shifted to mobile.
- Marketing communications industry: areas (advertisers/clients, agencies, media, suppliers, regulators, measurement companies) work together to form an industry that is ethical, trustworthy, cohesive, and measurable.
- Regulatory bodies: Advertising Standards Canada (ASC — adstandards.ca; administers the Canadian Code of Advertising Standards; self-regulation); Competition Bureau (enforces the Competition Act — misleading advertising, deceptive marketing); Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC — regulates broadcasting and telecom; CASL anti-spam enforcement); Canadian Marketing Association (CMA — code of ethics); Canadian Wireless Telecommunications Association (CWTA — wireless/mobile industry); Mobile Marketing Association (MMA — mobile marketing guidelines).
- Storytelling: builds relationships by conveying events using images and words; stories engage people through their emotions and experiences, which motivates them to act and to more readily accept a concept or idea (Dove example).
- Internal marketing: the role of internal marketing (employees/people) as a key to competitive advantage; emerging new role: employer brand manager; use of social media for engagement and development of content (ultimate goal is to create experiences).

## Core terms (textbook)
- Promotional mix / marketing communications mix elements (textbook): advertising (any paid form of non-personal communication about an organization, product, service, or idea by an identified sponsor; mass; strengths: reach, control of message; weaknesses: cost, hard to get feedback), personal selling (two-way flow of communication between buyer and seller designed to influence a purchase decision; customized; strengths: immediate feedback, persuasive, tailored; weaknesses: cost per contact high), public relations (form of communication management that seeks to influence feelings, opinions, beliefs of customers, stakeholders; publicity = non-personal, indirectly paid presentation — news stories; strengths: credibility; weaknesses: little control), sales promotion (short-term inducement of value offered to arouse interest in buying: coupons, samples, contests, rebates, trade promotions; strengths: effective at changing behaviour short-term; weaknesses: easily abused, price wars), direct marketing (uses direct communication with consumers to generate a response — direct mail, catalogues, telemarketing, direct response TV, email; strengths: measurable, targeted; weaknesses: cost, privacy), digital/social media/online, event marketing and sponsorship, product placement.
- Hierarchy of effects: the sequence of stages a prospective buyer goes through — awareness, interest, evaluation, trial, adoption.
- Promotional budgeting methods: percentage of sales (fixed % of past or anticipated sales; simple but backwards — sales determine promotion), competitive parity (match competitors' spending or share of market; ignores own objectives), all-you-can-afford (money left after other budget items; common in small firms), objective and task (best: set objectives, outline tasks, determine cost — but requires judgment).
- Customer touch points: all the points of contact between a customer and a brand (ads, packaging, store, website, service calls) considered when identifying the target audience.
- Target audience: the group of prospective buyers toward which a promotional program is directed; knowing the audience (demographics, lifestyle, media habits) is key.
- Promotional schedule: describes the order in which each promotional tool is introduced and the frequency/duration of its use; scheduling the IMC elements involves decisions about timing of ads/placements. Approaches: continuous (steady), flighting (intermittent periods of activity and none), pulse (continuous base plus periodic bursts).
- Program evaluation: pretests (before running) and posttests (after) of promotional elements; metrics — sales data, customer feedback, digital engagement (click-through, conversions, engagement rates); key challenge: coordinating a consistent promotional effort across departments responsible for different elements.
- Paid media (channels a company pays to use), owned media (channels the company controls at least in part — website, blog, social profiles, apps), earned media (publicity/word of mouth generated by others — reviews, shares, press coverage).
- Agencies: full-service agencies (research, creative, media, production), limited-service/specialty agencies (creative boutiques, media buying), in-house agencies.
- Measurement/metrics and analytics are integral to IMC today.

## Instructor practice questions (official, with answers)
1. Ginger Inc. focuses communications on consumers to build awareness and create demand = PULL strategy.
2. FoodMart membership card with coupons, draws, discounts for continued purchases = LOYALTY stage of the advocacy funnel (CRM programs reward continued purchases).
3. Brand regularly updates its official blog and social media profiles = OWNED media.
4. Defining what to achieve (brand awareness, sales) before a campaign = SPECIFYING THE IMC OBJECTIVES (step 1).
5. The PLC also helps marketers in CHOOSING THE APPROPRIATE COMMUNICATION TOOLS at each phase (promotional mix composition changes by phase).
6. Maturity stage objective = MAINTAIN EXISTING BUYERS with reminder advertising and sales promotions.
7. Deciding specific timing of TV ads and online video placements = SCHEDULING THE IMC ELEMENTS (step 5).
8. Key challenge in assembling the promotional mix = COORDINATING A CONSISTENT PROMOTIONAL EFFORT ACROSS VARIOUS DEPARTMENTS responsible for different elements.
9. Incentives and training to mobile phone retailers and sales staff = push strategy: INFLUENCE THE DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL to support and sell the product.
10. Reviewing sales data, feedback, digital engagement after a campaign = EVALUATING THE PROGRAM AND RECOMMENDING CHANGES (step 6).

## Traps / confusables
- Encoding (sender translates idea into symbols) vs decoding (receiver interprets) vs noise vs feedback vs field of experience.
- Selective inattention (tuning out while exposed) vs selective inexposure (avoiding exposure altogether) vs poor awareness vs personal bias.
- Push (to the trade: retailers/wholesalers; personal selling, trade promotion, incentives, training, allowances) vs pull (to consumers: advertising, consumer promotion, publicity, social media). Incentives to retailers' sales staff = push.
- Advocacy funnel order: awareness -> interest -> engagement -> trial -> purchase -> loyalty -> advocacy. Loyalty = repeat purchases/rewards; advocacy = recommending to others; engagement = participating/interacting; trial = samples/limited trial.
- Six IMC process steps: objectives -> target audience -> budget -> design program -> schedule & run -> evaluate. Touch points inform step 2; PLC informs step 4.
- PLC promotional objectives: inform (intro) -> persuade (growth) -> remind (maturity) -> phase out (decline). Maturity = reminder advertising + sales promotion to maintain buyers (NOT reduce spending to maximize profit — that's decline).
- Communication objectives (awareness, attitudes, relationships) vs behavioural objectives (trial, purchase, action).
- Inbound (content, search, be found; cost-effective) vs outbound (traditional push-out).
- Paid (search/display/social ads, paid influencers, email marketing) vs owned (website, own social pages, app, blog) vs earned (reviews, PR coverage, shares). Email marketing is classed as PAID on the course slide; company blog/social profiles = owned.
- Advertising (paid, non-personal, identified sponsor) vs publicity (non-personal, indirectly paid, not controlled) vs sales promotion (short-term inducement) vs personal selling (two-way, customized) vs direct marketing (direct response).
- Budget methods: percentage of sales vs competitive parity vs all-you-can-afford vs objective-and-task (best).
- Regulators: ASC (ad standards code, self-regulation) vs Competition Bureau (Competition Act, misleading ads) vs CRTC (broadcast/telecom) vs CMA (marketing code of ethics) vs CWTA (wireless industry) vs MMA (mobile marketing).
- Internal marketing (employees as audience; employer brand manager) vs external IMC.
